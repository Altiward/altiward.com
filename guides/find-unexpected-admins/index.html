Find unexpected Mac admin accounts

Altiward's free Unexpected admins check lists local admins outside your permanent-admin list. Use it as a Jamf Pro extension attribute or adapt its output for Intune. It gives you a snapshot to investigate; it does not verify whether rights were granted.

unexpected-admins.zshMIT licenceJamf Pro · IntunePublished October 2026

A local admin outside your permanent list might hold approved temporary rights, be a leftover from a previous setup, or need further investigation. This script cannot tell those cases apart. Review its results against your approved accounts and grants before taking action.

As a Jamf Pro extension attribute, it reports at each inventory update so you can group Macs with accounts to review.

What it checks

  • Every local account on the Mac, including hidden ones with a UID below 500. Only macOS's own service accounts (names starting with an underscore, plus daemon and nobody) are skipped.
  • Whether each account is in the admin group, using dseditgroup -o checkmember. That also catches accounts made admin through a nested group, which reading the group's member list alone would miss.
  • Whether the account is on your list of permanent admins. Those are left out of the result.

It reports None, or a comma-separated list of account names. The person logged in at the Mac is marked (logged in).

The script

Download unexpected-admins.zsh or copy it from here. It's MIT licensed, so you can change it and use it however you like. Set PERMANENT_ADMINS to your own permanent admin accounts before you deploy it.

Add it to Jamf Pro

  1. In Jamf Pro, go to Settings, then Computer management, then Extension attributes, and click New.
  2. Name it Unexpected admins. Set the data type to String and the input type to Script.
  3. Paste in the script, with your PERMANENT_ADMINS list, and save.
  4. Create a smart computer group called Macs with unexpected admins, with the criterion Unexpected admins is not None.

Results arrive as each Mac next submits inventory. If you'd rather manage the list of permanent admins with a configuration profile than edit the script, set PREF_DOMAIN to your profile's preference domain and put the names in a PermanentAdmins array.

Using Intune instead

Intune's custom attributes for macOS also run a shell script and report what it prints. Change the two echo lines at the end so they print the result without the <result> tags.

Reading the results

NoneNo admin outside your permanent list when inventory ran.
jdoe (logged in)The person at the Mac. Expected if they had temporary admin through SAP Privileges when inventory ran; they'll drop off the list once their rights end. If they show up on every inventory update, they're probably a permanent admin nobody moved to standard user.
support2Outside your permanent-admin list and not the current console user. That doesn't establish whether its rights are authorised. Check the account's purpose and grant history before deciding whether to remove its rights or update your list.

What a script can't tell you

An extension attribute is a snapshot, taken whenever inventory runs, often once a day. An account made admin at 10:00 and removed at 10:20 never shows up. The script also runs on a Mac where the person may hold admin rights, so someone determined could stop it reporting them. And it can't tell you whether an admin was granted, only whether the account is on your list.
