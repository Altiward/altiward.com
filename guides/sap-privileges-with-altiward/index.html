What Altiward adds to SAP Privileges

Add expiry enforcement, unexpected-admin findings and fleet-wide session evidence to your existing elevation workflow. Compare SAP Privileges alone with SAP Privileges plus Altiward.

Last reviewed October 2026SAP Privileges 2.6.0

Altiward checks what happens after admin is granted. It compares recorded grants with the Mac's admin group, can remove rights found past expiry, and gives your team a fleet view, console revocation and SIEM findings. SAP Privileges continues to handle elevation.

Get Altiward on GitHub, or see the console and example events.

Compare SAP Privileges audit history feature

Privileges 2.6.0 added an audit history. On macOS 14 or later, with its system extension on, it records each change to the admin group, including changes made outside Privileges, and the process that made it. The --history option of PrivilegesCLI shows the past 30 days of entries written by 2.6.0 or later. That answers what happened on one Mac. The questions below are about every Mac.

QuestionPrivileges 2.6 alonePrivileges 2.6 + Altiward
Where is the record kept?In the Mac's own log, read with PrivilegesCLI --history for the past 30 daysOn your server, for as long as you choose, and in your SIEM
Who can erase it?Anyone with admin rights on that Mac, including the person it records. Wiping, replacing or losing the Mac erases it tooNo one on the Mac: the record is on your server and in your SIEM
What reaches your SIEM?Remote logging sends the grants and revocations Privileges makes. Admin-group changes made outside Privileges, and attempts the extension blocked, stay on the MacEvery admin nobody granted, with the process that added them, each attempt the extension blocked, and every grant and revocation

Rights that outlive the timerA grant with no revocation after it, found by reading each Mac's historyThe agent reads the admin group and raises an alert, or removes the rights with ExpiryEnforcement set to enforce

A quick test: from one place, without signing in to any Mac, can you list everyone who holds admin now, say which of them nobody granted, and show that last quarter's grants were removed? Privileges 2.6 records the facts on each Mac. Altiward collects them, checks them against the admin group and keeps them off the Mac.

If grants and revocations in your SIEM are enough, altiward-forwarder relays Privileges' webhook with no agent on the Mac. It carries what remote logging sends, so changes made outside Privileges still stay on the Mac.

Check the admin group after expiry

A timer and a grant event describe the intended admin window. Altiward's agent also reads the Mac's admin group. With enforcement enabled, it removes rights found past the recorded expiry and configured grace period, then records enforcement as the reason the session ended. The default policy alerts without removing rights; the example agent profile documents the options.

Privileges has its own revocation settings and optional tamper protection. Keep those configured: Altiward adds another check rather than making the original controls unnecessary. Our expiry guide explains the documented limits and settings. Altiward records and revokes admin rights but doesn't stop anyone asking Privileges again: to do that, change Privileges' settings, as Stop someone getting admin explains.

Bring unexpected admins into view

Altiward checks for admin membership without a recorded grant and outside your list of permanent admins. It also flags an admin account detected during an approved session. Your team can review the finding, request revocation from the console and correlate the session with other security events.

With SAP Privileges 2.6 or later and its system extension on, Altiward also reads what the extension records on the Mac: which process added the account to the admin group, and any attempt the extension blocked to remove or move Privileges. Privileges keeps both on the Mac; Altiward puts them in the session's timeline, the Mac's alerts and your SIEM. It also checks each grant Privileges reports against Privileges' own log, so a grant claimed by running Altiward's hook by hand doesn't count: in enforce mode it's refused and the rights are removed, and either way it's reported.

These findings identify something to investigate. An account without a recorded grant is not, by itself, proof of malicious activity.

Keep control of the evidence

Your MDM deploys the Altiward agent and configuration alongside Privileges. You run the Altiward server and console on your infrastructure and choose where to send its session records and findings. See the example session, console and SIEM event.
